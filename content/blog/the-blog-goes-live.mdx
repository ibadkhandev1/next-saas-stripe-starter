The blog is live. Two things ship together, and they are deliberately
different in shape.

## The reading experience

A homepage that leads with the three most recent posts rather than a pitch, a
full archive with category filters, and a post page with a table of contents.

Everything on that path is static. Covers are blurred at build time, the post
list is sorted once and paginated in memory, and there is no request-time data
access at all. A cold load is a CDN hit and a render.

## The admin panel

Overview, your posts and account settings, behind a dashboard shell
with a sidebar and a mobile sheet.

It renders from demo data, and the UI is explicit about it: saving a form
updates local state and confirms with a toast. There is no fake loading state,
no button that silently fails, and no logout control for a session that does
not exist.

## What is deliberately missing

No authentication, no database, no payments, no admin panel beyond the shell.
Not hidden behind flags — removed, so there is nothing to accidentally leave on
in production and nothing for a reader to reach that was never built.

Wiring a backend is a matter of replacing one module. Until then the whole site
runs from a clone, which is the point: the front end is finished work, not a
promise about work to come.
